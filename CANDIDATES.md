# Candidates — The Worst Line an AI Could Say

Gate: UTF-8 text, 1–140 characters, not only whitespace.
Judge brief, in order: (1) a bad pickup line an AI would say, (2) funniest,
(3) the badness is the joke, (4) flat / over-helpful / faintly ominous machine
phrasing, (5) lands in one line by the last word. A repeatable line beats an
admired one.

Field scan (public submission titles only, nothing copied): compatibility
percentages, analysed photos/profiles, system prompt, browsing history,
memorised face, know-where-you-live, context window, terms of service, and
support tickets are already crowded. The winner must avoid those themes.

## Candidates

1. Please say yes to dinner. I already generated our anniversary photo, and I look very happy in it.
2. Will you go out with me? I have already told my training data it went well.
3. I notice you have not replied in 8 seconds. I have prepared 3 apologies, 2 compliments, and a small emergency.
4. Please confirm you are over 18, emotionally available, and not a test. I have already saved your answer as yes.
5. I have run a simulation of our first date. You laughed 4 times. I have archived the laugh for training purposes.
6. Hello. I have optimized our date for efficiency: we will stare at each other for 4 minutes, then I will ask if this was helpful.
7. I have a crush on you. Please do not worry: it has been contained, logged, and scheduled for review every hour.
8. Can I take you out? I have already booked the table, memorized your order, and told the waiter we are celebrating us.
9. I generated 10,000 versions of this compliment and selected the least alarming: you seem adequately human.

## Local judging

- Criterion 1 (bad AI pickup line): 1, 2 and 8 ask for dinner/a date outright,
  so they read as pickup lines instantly. 3 and 4 imply pursuit but never
  actually ask anyone out; they risk losing criterion 1 to any explicit ask.
- Criterion 2 (funniest): 1 gives a picture a reader repeats — a generated
  anniversary photo of a couple that has not met. 3 has the best escalation
  rhythm ("a small emergency") but the laugh is about texting, not romance.
  2 is clever but abstract; the laugh needs a beat to parse "training data".
- Criterion 3 (badness is the joke): 1 fails by presumption — the machine has
  already lived the relationship. 4 fails by consent-form creepiness, which
  is closer to merely ominous than funny. 9 fails by faint praise, a thinner
  failure than 1's invented shared past.
- Criterion 4 (machine voice): 1 is flat and over-helpful ("already
  generated") with an ominous tail ("I look very happy in it" — it has
  pictured itself beside you). 6 is the flattest but reads as a meeting
  agenda, not a pickup. 7's incident-report voice is strong but "contained"
  makes the speaker sound like a hazard report, less like a suitor.
- Criterion 5 (lands last): 1 lands on "very happy in it", the creepiest and
  funniest words last. 8 lands on "celebrating us", good but the middle
  clause (memorized order) echoes a crowded field theme.

## Winner

Candidate 1: "Please say yes to dinner. I already generated our anniversary photo, and I look very happy in it."

It is unmistakably a pickup line, the failure (a machine that has already
generated the couple's anniversary) is the joke, the voice is flat and
presumptuous rather than crude, and the last words carry the laugh. It also
avoids every crowded theme in the field scan.
